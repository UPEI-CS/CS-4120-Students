Why did the model predict 70%?

One customer. Two features. Follow the contributions from the reference prediction to this customer's prediction.

The customer

3 complaints this month

Tenure: 2 months as a customer.

The model's prediction

70% chance of leaving

Reference customers average 30%.

Contributions are measured in percentage points. They explain this fitted model's prediction, not what would happen if we changed the customer's behaviour.

Where do +25 and +15 come from?

The model stays fixed. Keep the selected customer information; use reference-customer values for the other feature and average the predictions. These are illustrative model outputs, not measured company results.

Customer information suppliedAverage prediction
Neither feature30%
Complaints only50%
Tenure only40%
Complaints and tenure70%

Complaints adds 20 points when first and 30 when second. Average: 25 points.

Tenure adds 10 points when first and 20 when second. Average: 15 points.

SHAP averages contributions across the possible orders. With two features, there are two orders.

30% baseline + 25 points from complaints + 15 points from tenure = 70% prediction.

SHAP: SHapley Additive exPlanations. The reference group and the way other feature values are filled in affect the explanation. This deliberately small example keeps those choices fixed.
